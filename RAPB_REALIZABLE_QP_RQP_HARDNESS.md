# Hardness of Realizable Decision-List Bicriteria Approximation

This report proves the deterministic lower bound under $\mathrm{NP}\nsubseteq\mathrm{QP}$ and the randomized lower bound under $\mathrm{NP}\nsubseteq\mathrm{RQP}$. Both hold at every fixed error target strictly below $1/2$, on the promise that $k$ rules suffice for zero error. The only external hardness theorem used is the perfect-completeness $4$-to-$1$ Games theorem. The reduction from that theorem to decision lists is proved here.

## 1. Model and theorem

The input is an explicit indexed sample $S=((x_j,y_j))_{j=1}^N$ with $x_j\in\{0,1\}^d$ and $y_j\in\{0,1\}$. Equal vectors must have equal labels; repeated indices count separately toward empirical error. A native rule is a conjunction of signed coordinate literals with a bit label. A list predicts the label of the first matching rule, or the bit on its one final default. The default is free; $|L|$ counts all other rules. Write $\operatorname{OPT}_k(S)$ for the minimum empirical error over lists with at most $k$ paid rules and one default. The input supplies $S$ and $k$, not a realizing list or any auxiliary structure.

Here $\mathrm{QP}$ is deterministic time $2^{(\log(v+2))^{O(1)}}$ on inputs of length $v$. We use $\mathrm{RQP}$ for the same worst-case time with one-sided randomization: NO inputs are always rejected, and YES inputs are accepted with probability at least $2/3$.

**Theorem.** Fix $0<\varepsilon<1/2$. There exists $c_\varepsilon>0$ such that, given $S,k$ with $d\le\log_2 N$ and $\operatorname{OPT}_k(S)=0$, returning a native list $L$ satisfying

$$
\operatorname{err}_S(L)\le \varepsilon,
\qquad |L|\le k\exp\!\big(c_\varepsilon(\ln(N+2))^{1/6}\big)
$$

is impossible in deterministic quasipolynomial time under $\mathrm{NP}\nsubseteq\mathrm{QP}$. Under $\mathrm{NP}\nsubseteq\mathrm{RQP}$, it is impossible in randomized quasipolynomial time with probability at least $2/3$ of meeting **both** guarantees on every promised input. In particular, both lower bounds apply to every fixed length multiplier $A(\log_2(N+2))^C$, relative to $k$, for constants $A,C>0$.

For the realizable accuracy-parameterized bicriteria (RAPB) target, fixing $\varepsilon$ makes $2^{\operatorname{poly}(1/\varepsilon)}$ a constant. Thus the theorem rules out the polynomial-time, $2^{\operatorname{poly}(1/\varepsilon)}k\operatorname{polylog}N$-length target in the respective models. It also rules out error zero by implication. At error $1/2$, the majority default alone works, so the strict endpoint in the theorem is necessary.

## 2. Source and address encodings

**External theorem.** By [Fei, Minzer and Wang, *On the Hardness of 4-to-1 Games with Perfect Completeness*, Theorem 1.6 and Definitions 1.1 and 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12378v2), for every fixed $\delta>0$ a deterministic polynomial-time reduction from SAT gives a bipartite game of constant alphabet sizes. An edge occurrence has a total projection from the left alphabet to the right alphabet, exactly four-to-one. The value is the largest fraction of edge occurrences satisfied by a vertex labeling. A satisfiable input gives value one; an unsatisfiable input gives value at most $\delta$. Edges below are sampled uniformly by occurrence, including when several occurrences have the same endpoints.

**Smooth group game.** Discard isolated vertices. Take disjoint copies if needed so the edge count is at least the SAT input length $v$. Draw $h$ independent original edges $(u_j,v_j)$ and a uniform $i\in[h]$. Give the left player $(u_1,\ldots,u_h)$ and the right player $(i,v_i,(u_j)_{j\ne i})$. The answers are label vectors, with a right-alphabet label in position $i$ on the right, and left-alphabet labels everywhere else. Project position $i$ along its original edge and copy all other labels. The projection remains exactly $b$-to-one with $b=4$. Completeness is still one. For soundness, fix $i$ and the other left vertices. The two strategies' answers in position $i$ give a strategy for the original game on edge $i$; success in the group game implies success there. The NO value therefore stays at most $\delta$. For any two distinct left labels at a fixed left vertex, only one choice of $i$ could make them collide, so their projection-collision probability is at most $\gamma=1/h$. We can enlarge the right alphabet size $R$ arbitrarily by attaching a common label index on both sides and copying that index under the projection. This preserves $b$, completeness, soundness, and smoothness. Let $q=bR$ be the resulting left alphabet size. The group game has a polynomial number of occurrences in $v$ for fixed parameters.

**Semantic copies and balanced codes.** On each side, give a vertex as many degree copies as its occurrence degree, and sample its copy uniformly. Independently attach a uniform alphabet permutation $P\in S_q$ on the left or $T\in S_R$ on the right. Add harmless uniform dummy indices to make the counts of semantic copies equal on the two sides: for example, use $R!$ possible dummy values on the left and $q!$ on the right. At a uniform edge the semantic endpoint on each side is uniform on its own set of copies. Conditional on the original edge, all the copy, permutation, dummy and subsequent coset draws are independent across the two sides. On these raw field indices the projection is $\rho=T^{-1}\pi P$, where $\pi$ is the projection of the underlying group occurrence.

Let $n=\lceil C_n\log_2(v+2)\rceil$, where the constant can be increased after all game and taper constants are fixed. Choose nested binary linear spaces $D\subset F\subset\mathbb F_2^n$ of dimensions $\lfloor n/4\rfloor$ and $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. They can be chosen so both $F$ and $D^\perp$ have minimum distance greater than $2\alpha n$ for an absolute $\alpha>0$. Indeed, for a uniformly random nested pair each subspace has the uniform marginal at its dimension. The probability a fixed nonzero vector belongs to $F$ or $D^\perp$ is respectively $O(2^{-n/2})$ or $O(2^{-n/4})$. Choose $\alpha$ so small that the count of vectors of weight at most $2\alpha n$ is $2^{o_\alpha(1)n}$ with exponent less than $1/4$; the usual binomial bound or summing $\binom{n}{j}\le(en/j)^j$ gives this. A union bound proves existence for large $n$. Enumerating binary generators and checking distances costs $2^{O(n^2)}$ time.

Give each semantic endpoint copy a distinct coset of $D$ in $F$, separately for each side. The quotient has $2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor-\lfloor n/4\rfloor}$ cosets, enough by the polynomial bound on copies and the choice of $C_n$. Draw a uniform word of the assigned coset; **draw the two coset words independently given the edge and both semantic copies**. On any at most $\alpha n$ underlying coordinates of one coset the draw is exactly uniform: failure of surjectivity on those coordinates would give a nonzero word of $D^\perp$ supported there. Encode each bit $x$ by $(x,1-x)$. A full address is the concatenation of these balanced words over $t$ independent groups. Each side's address is uniform on its set of $z$ valid full addresses; $\log_2 z=\Theta(tn)$. Every address has exactly $tn$ zeros. Distinct valid full addresses differ on at least an $\alpha/t$ fraction of their bits, since a differing group word comes from $F$. Validity always refers to the full address set of the relevant side.

## 3. The sample and the zero-error witness

Use $t=\lceil\log_2(v+2)\rceil$ groups in each pair and $m$ pairs, with

$$
m=\lceil C_m t^2(1+\log_2 z)\rceil.
$$

The constant $C_m$ will depend on the desired gap. Each pair has two endpoints, each with a full address, an activation bit, and rank fields for its $t$ groups. A raw field is a pair of binary words. Fix a sufficiently large power of two $H$ and set $Q=H-5$. A pair active in mode $y\in\{0,1\}$ has the following law.

1. Choose independent group edges, semantic endpoint copies and coset words as above. Both activation bits are one. Independently choose baselines $B_{Li},B_{Ri}\in\{0,\ldots,5\}$ uniformly among vectors satisfying $\sum_{i=1}^t(B_{Li}-B_{Ri})=y$.
2. In group $i$ each right field $a\in[R]$ receives an independent jitter $J_{ia}\in\{0,\ldots,Q-1\}$. Let $L_H=\lceil(\log_2 H)^2\rceil$. The jitter mass at $j$ is proportional to $(1+1/L_H)^{\min(j,Q-1-j,\lfloor Q/10\rfloor)}$. Consecutive atom ratios are in $[(1+1/L_H)^{-1},1+1/L_H]$. The minimum atom occurs at either endpoint, with common mass $p\le\exp(-\Omega(H/(\log H)^2))$, by comparing with a plateau atom.
3. The right rank at field $a$ is $B_{Ri}+J_{ia}$; the left rank at field $f\in[q]$ is $B_{Li}+J_{i,\rho_i(f)}$. To publish a rank $r\in\{0,\ldots,H-1\}$, independently at each raw field draw a uniform mask $U\in\{0,\ldots,H-1\}$ and publish the $\log_2 H$ bits of $U$ and of $(U+r)\bmod H$. Either word alone is uniform conditional on the rank. A field packet conditional on its baseline has minimum positive atom at least $p/H$.

An inactive pair starts as an independent active pair with fair random mode. On each endpoint independently apply one of two changes, each with probability $1/2$: clear its activation bit, or flip a uniform zero address bit to one. In the latter case the address is invalid because it is unbalanced. Leave the rest of the endpoint unchanged. To generate a row, choose a uniform served pair and fair target label $y$, draw that pair active in mode $y$, and independently draw all other pairs inactive. Call this the ideal law. It is only a description of a finite distribution to be enumerated, not a randomized reduction.

**Consistency, including in NO.** The served pair is uniquely identifiable from the Boolean row: both its endpoints have activation one and valid addresses. Decode its ranks from the two published words. In each group the mean left rank minus the mean right rank is $B_{Li}-B_{Ri}$, since every right jitter occurs exactly $b$ times on the left. Summing gives $y$. Therefore no vector is assigned both labels, even if it arises from different latent choices.

**YES witness.** A satisfying group-game labeling determines one selected raw field per group at each endpoint, from the semantic copy and its permutation. Thus the choice is determined by the endpoint's full valid address. For every pair, each endpoint and each of its $z$ full addresses, include a rule for each of the $H^{2t}$ possible assignments to the two published words of the $t$ selected fields. The rule tests those words, that exact address, and activation one. Label left rules zero and right rules one. Sort all these rules by the sum of the decoded ranks on the tested fields, breaking ties in favor of left rules. Inactive endpoints cannot match any rule for their pair. On the served pair precisely one rule per endpoint matches, and the selected jitters on the two sides agree because the game labels satisfy the edge. The left sum minus the right sum is $y$: the left rule comes first for $y=0$ and the right for $y=1$. One arbitrary default completes a zero-error list with

$$
k=2mzH^{2t}
$$

paid rules. In particular the YES promise is exactly zero empirical optimum, not an almost-perfect approximation to it.

## 4. Soundness for arbitrary native lists

Fix a NO input and any list with $J\le k2^t$ paid rules. All estimates below are uniform over such lists. The *owner* is the first matching rule, counting the free default last. Let $\zeta>0$ be a fixed small slack. We first show that in a coupled comparison of the active modes, the first matching rule of a particular syntactically defined class is usually the same. We then transfer that statement to the full owners. No list order or block locality is assumed in either argument.

### 4.1. Adjacent baseline slices and narrow matching owners

Replace each right baseline by $5-B_{Ri}$. The two baseline slices now have sums $5t$ and $5t+1$ in $\{0,\ldots,5\}^{2t}$. Couple them by a uniform increment edge from the lower slice to the upper slice. At either endpoint there are between $t$ and $2t$ eligible coordinates; conditional on that endpoint, the increment or reverse decrement coordinate is uniform among them. In particular any group is moved with probability at most $2/t$ conditional on either endpoint.

The endpoint distributions of this coupling differ from the uniform slice distributions by $o(1)$ in total variation. To see this, the probabilities are proportional to eligible degree. With unrestricted independent uniform entries, the degree (number of coordinates below five, or number above zero) is binomial with mean $5t/3$. Its deviation beyond $t^{3/4}$ has probability $\exp(-\Omega(\sqrt t))$ by a bounded-sum tail bound. The center slice has probability $\Omega(t^{-1/2})$ by concentration in an $O(\sqrt t)$ window and the fact that the symmetric unimodal sum distribution has maximum at the center. Unimodality follows, for example, because convolution with an interval uniform preserves symmetry and unimodality: compare adjacent moving sums of the symmetric unimodal mass function. The upper slice has at least half the center slice probability by counting increment edges and using the degree bounds. So the eligible degrees still concentrate after conditioning on either slice, and the normalized size bias changes each slice by $o(1)$.

Call a paid rule *charged for the served pair* if on at least one of its two endpoints it tests activation one and its address query matches exactly one valid full address. Assign such a rule one of these endpoints and addresses as a syntactically fixed anchor for that pair. The *charged owner* is the first matching charged rule, using a sentinel if there is none. A rule can have different anchors for different served pairs. Its being charged does not restrict its other tests, including those on inactive pairs.

On the two actual full addresses in the served pair, the expected sum of the sizes of the corresponding anchor lists is at most $J/z$, since each charged rule has only one anchor. Put $D_0=\lceil(J/z+1)e^t\rceil$. By Markov's inequality an actual anchor is heavier than $D_0$ with probability $O(e^{-t})$. Because $\log m=o(t)$ and $J\le 2mzH^{2t}2^t$, we have $\log_2(D_0+1)\le 2t\log_2 H+O(t)$.

For an anchored rule count one variable for each distinct raw packet tested in the served pair, and one for each underlying address bit tested at the opposite endpoint, merging complementary encodings. Ignore the exact own anchor, inactive pairs and activation bits in this count. Contradictory tests can be dropped as nonmatching. Set $D_*=\lceil10t(1+\log_2 H)\rceil$ and choose $C_n$ so $D_*<\alpha n$. A wider rule passes with conditional probability at most $2^{-D_*}$: choose one tested literal from each of $D_*$ distinct counted variables. Conditional on the semantic endpoints, own full address, baselines and jitters, the packet literals are independent fair tests by the independent masks. The opposite address literals are also independent fair tests by the coset property, independent of those packets. Lightness of the own anchor depends only on that anchor and cannot expose the opposite coset. A union bound over the rules in the two light anchor lists shows that, off $o(1)$ mass, no matching charged rule has width exceeding $D_*$. Filter to this width syntactically.

Also, for any fixed $\lambda>0$, off $o(1)$ mass no width-filtered rule at a light actual anchor tests endpoint jitters $0,Q-1$ in at least $\lceil\lambda t\rceil$ distinct groups. For a fixed rule and this many chosen fields in distinct groups the probability is $(2p)^{\lceil\lambda t\rceil}$, by independence of the jitters between groups conditional on the edge and both addresses. The choices are bounded by $2^{D_*}$, giving a union bound $2D_0 2^{D_*}(2p)^{\lceil\lambda t\rceil}=o(1)$ for sufficiently large fixed $H$.

Let $g$ denote the moved group, $s=\lceil24(1+\log_2 H)/\zeta\rceil$, and $\lambda=\zeta/4$. The coupling below preserves the conditional law of the latent row given the baseline pair and move for each marginal. Thus conditional on a full latent row the moved group still has probability at most $2/t$ per group. The charged owner, when not a sentinel, is therefore at a light anchor, has total width at most $D_*$, has at most $s$ variables in $g$, and tests no endpoint jitter in $g$, except on at most $2\zeta+o(1)$ mass in either mode. Here at most $D_*/s$ groups exceed the local width cutoff and fewer than $\lceil\lambda t\rceil$ have an endpoint jitter, off the exceptional events just bounded. The bounds apply also under the degree-biased slice marginals.

### 4.2. A bounded local subsequence and the crossed-index bound

**Local subsequence lemma.** Suppose each test of a list reads at most $s$ variables, with a single nonempty allowed set per read variable. There are at most $K_0$ different possible allowed sets per variable. Under a product law of finite variables with minimum positive atom $\mu>0$, for any $\xi>0$ there is a subsequence of at most $P_*(s,K_0,\mu,\xi)$ tests preserving the first owner except on mass $\xi$. Tests with zero reference probability can be omitted if they are impossible under the true law as well. The guarantee only needs the variables of the retained tests to have the stated product law; the discarded variables can have arbitrary dependencies.

*Proof.* Remove later exact duplicates. Scan the remaining list. Discard a test if some subtest of it is the common labeled core of $r_*$ earlier retained tests whose supports outside the core are pairwise disjoint. Otherwise keep it. At most $C_{s,K_0}r_*^s$ tests are retained. For each support size $a$, more than $a!(r-1)^a$ distinct supports contain $r$ with pairwise intersection the same core: a maximal disjoint subfamily either has $r$ members or its union has at most $a(r-1)$ elements meeting every set, so restrict to a frequent element, remove it and induct on $a$. Apply this count with $r>r_*K_0^s$ and select one retained test per support. More than $r_*$ of the resulting tests agree on the predicates in the core, so the last of those could not have been retained. There are at most $K_0^s$ tests per support. Now if a discarded test owns a row, its core passes and all petals of the first completed witnessing sunflower for that labeled core fail. Conditional on the core, the disjoint petals independently pass with probability at least $\mu^s$; an empty petal always passes. There are at most $2^s C_{s,K_0}r_*^s$ possible cores, all subtests of retained witnesses. The union bound by this count times $(1-\mu^s)^{r_*}$ is below $\xi$ for sufficiently large $r_*$. Only retained variables were used in the probability bound. If the owner is retained, deleting other tests does not change it. $\square$

**Crossed indices.** Fix a $b$-to-one $\pi:[q]\to[R]$ with $q=bR$. For independent uniform permutations $P,T$, let $j\in[R]$ depend on $P$ and private randomness on its side, and let $i\in[q]$ depend on $T$ and independent private randomness on its side. Then

$$
\Pr[\pi(P(i))=T(j)] = O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{b+\log(qR+2)}{R}}\right).
$$

*Proof.* Write $p_i$ for the probability of choice $i$. Choices with $p_i\ge u$ contribute at most $1/(Ru)$: for each such fixed $i$, condition on the left randomness and ignore the right choice event, using uniformity of $T(j)$ without conditioning on that choice. For $p_i<u$, condition on $j$ and use independence between sides to bound the contribution by $\sum_{i:p_i<u}p_i\max_a\Pr[\pi(P(i))=a\mid j]$. Let $a_{ij}$ attain the maximum, and put $Z_j(P)=|\{i:\pi(P(i))=a_{ij}\}|$. The light contribution averaged over $j$ is at most $u\,\mathbb E_P\max_j Z_j(P)$. For $\ell=\lceil10\log_2(qR+2)\rceil\le R/2$, any prescription on $\ell$ distinct input slots has probability at most $b^\ell/(q)_\ell\le(2/R)^\ell$, where $(q)_\ell=q(q-1)\cdots(q-\ell+1)$. Counting $\ell$-subsets of the matches gives

$$
\Pr[\max_j Z_j\ge a]\le R\binom{q}{\ell}(2/R)^\ell/\binom{a}{\ell}.
$$

For $a\ge\ell+16eb$ this is at most $R4^{-\ell}$, by the upper and lower bounds $(eq/\ell)^\ell$ and $((a-\ell+1)/\ell)^\ell$ on the respective binomial coefficients. Since $Z_j\le q$, the expectation is $O(b+\ell)$, also if this $a$ exceeds $q$. Choose $u=1/\sqrt{R(b+\ell)}$. Smaller $R$ are irrelevant to the construction, which can take $R$ arbitrarily large. $\square$

### 4.3. Coupling the charged owners

Use the adjacent baseline pair. The served index, inactive context, group edges, semantic clones and full addresses are common across modes, and all payloads outside the moved group are identical. Fix the outside-group frame and the baseline pair for the following analysis. The moved group's source is independent of the outside frame. In each of the two anchor lists, discard heavy anchors and restrict candidates to the total width bound and at most $s$ variables in the moved group. Evaluate tests outside that group, including all tests of inactive pairs. For the left-anchored bank the remaining syntactic list can depend on the left full address and outside frame but must not depend on the right moved-group address, which is still symbolic; reverse the roles for the right-anchored bank. Activation bits are fixed. The local variables are packets and foreign underlying address bits. Crucially, none of the lists are selected by observing either current local ranks or that bank's foreign moved-group address.

For each bank and each of the 36 possible pairs of moved-group baselines, apply the local subsequence lemma with $\xi=\zeta/2$. Its reference product has independent packets with the true one-field marginal and independent fair foreign address bits. All allowed sets are drawn from a fixed finite family depending on $H$, and the minimum positive atom is $\min(p/H,1/2)$. Impossible unary predicates can be removed since all true one-field marginals are the same. Take the union of the retained subsequences for each bank; one bank's union mentions at most $K=36sP_*$ slots or address bits. This bound depends on $H,\zeta$, not on the game alphabets or the outside frame.

We want distinct left slots **within each bank** to project to distinct classes, and no class used on the left by either bank to be used on the right by any bank. Right slots are already indexed by class. Conditional on the outside frame, a bank is determined by its own moved-group anchor alone. For each pair of slots in these bounded pools, the failure bounds are as follows.

| Slots being compared | Collision bound in NO |
| --- | --- |
| Two distinct left slots from the left bank | $\gamma$ by smoothness conditional on that original left vertex and its independent clone randomness. |
| Two distinct left slots from the right bank | $(b-1)/(q-1)$ by the fresh left permutation conditional on the underlying edge and right anchor. |
| A left slot from the left bank and a right slot from the right bank | $\delta$. Undoing the permutations gives a pair of strategies for the group game, each using only its own vertex and private clone randomness. |
| Opposite-side slots from the same bank | $1/R$ by regularity and the fresh opposite permutation. |
| A left slot from the right bank and a right slot from the left bank | The crossed-index bound, conditional on the underlying edge; the clone randomness on the two sides is independent. |

To apply these bounds, index the at most $K$ fields in each pool, using arbitrary filler slots for missing indices when needed. For within-bank comparisons use the bound only on events with two distinct slots; in the first row this condition depends only on the left strategy's own anchor, and in the second only on the right anchor. The NO value also bounds randomized private strategies by averaging over their private choices. Choose $\delta$ and $\gamma$ small after $H$ has fixed the pool bound, and then enlarge the alphabet with the copied index until the union of all collision events has probability at most $\zeta$.

To use the local subsequence lemma in **one** bank, condition on the edge, both semantic clones, its own full address and the outside frame, as well as the baseline pair. Under the event of this bank's within-bank injectivity (including no collision between its left and right fields), its retained packets are independent with the reference marginal laws. This event for that bank is already determined by the conditioning. Its foreign address bits remain independent fair bits by the coset property, since $K<\alpha n$ for sufficiently large $v$. Packets and foreign address bits are independent. Do not condition this step on the other bank's pool or on the cross-bank collision event: these may expose the foreign bits. The one-bank failure bound $\zeta/2$ is therefore valid on its own injectivity event. If the full charged owner is in an eligible local list, it is the first match in that list. Off the collision event and the previously bounded owner exceptions, the probability of losing that owner in the retained union is at most $\zeta$ per mode, by adding the two bank bounds.

To finish defining the coupling, now condition on both addresses as well as the edge, outside frame and baseline pair. If there is a collision in the retained pools, use any coupling with the correct conditional laws. Otherwise every jitter class named by the retained union is used on just one side, even if several slots on that side share it across banks. Independently for each such class, maximally couple the two ranks on its used side; infer the marginal jitter by subtracting the baseline. For other classes share the jitters. Use a common independent uniform $U$ in both rows at each raw field, separately across fields. Each marginal then has exactly the prescribed conditional law. If a used baseline moves, it moves by one. For a rank with nonendpoint jitter, the two laws at that rank have ratio between $(1+1/L_H)^{-1}$ and $1+1/L_H$. In a maximal coupling this bounds the conditional probability that the rank changes by $1/L_H$ from either marginal (allocate the smaller mass on the diagonal). This bound holds conditional on the full latent marginal row because the class couplings are independent, with choices made before observing the local ranks and masks. An unchanged rank with the common mask leaves the packet unchanged.

For a typical retained actual owner there are at most $s$ local tests, none on an endpoint jitter. Its tested fields change with probability at most $s/L_H\le\zeta$ by taking $H$ large. Its other tests are unchanged. If each charged owner that is not the sentinel still matches on the other row, the owners are identical by their common order, including the sentinel case. Counting collisions once and the two marginal owner exceptions, filter losses and packet-change losses gives at most $\zeta+2(2\zeta+\zeta+\zeta)+o(1)=9\zeta+o(1)$ disagreement under the degree-biased baseline laws. Adjust to the exact slices at total variation cost $o(1)$, without changing the shared served index, inactive context or group edge and address data. In particular a bound of $10\zeta+o(1)$ holds for the charged-owner disagreement under the ideal law.

### 4.4. Restoring inactive pairs controls the uncharged owners

For any context (served index and all other inactive blocks), take the coupled active rows of modes zero and one just constructed. Independently apply the inactive changes to the served block of each, producing restored rows $V^0,V^1$. Each restored block marginal, conditional on the context, has density at most two compared with the fair inactive mixture. The shared served edge and addresses are averaged out for this density comparison. Let $O$ be the full first owner, including the default. We will use two bounds:

$$
\Pr[O(V^0)\ne O(V^1)]\le 8\ln(J+1)/m,
\qquad
\Pr[O(V^y)\text{ fails on its active row}]\le 2\log_2(J+1)/m.
$$

For the first bound, on a draw from the product of all inactive block laws, let $p_j$ be the probability of owner $j$, and let $a_{j,i}$ be the probability rule $j$'s tests on block $i$ pass. The owner cell is contained in the match set, so $p_j\le\prod_i a_{j,i}$ and $\sum_i(1-a_{j,i})\le-\ln p_j$ when $p_j>0$. Resampling one independent uniform block loses the old owner's match with probability at most $\sum_j p_j(-\ln p_j)/m\le\ln(J+1)/m$. If owners change, the earlier owner fails in one of the two directions. By symmetry the disagreement probability is at most $2\ln(J+1)/m$. Conditional on the other blocks and the resampled index, let $p_*$ be the modal owner probability for the inactive mixture. Two independent draws then disagree with probability at least $1-p_*$, since the sum of squared owner probabilities is at most $p_*$. In either restored row the probability of differing from that context's modal owner is at most $2(1-p_*)$ by the density bound. Averaging and adding the two probabilities proves the first bound even for the dependent coupling.

For the second bound, first generate a product background from independent fair-mode active frames, then independently inactivate every pair. Conditional on all the active frames, let $f_j$ count the blocks on which rule $j$ fails before inactivation. On each failed block at least one endpoint fails. A conjunction failing at that endpoint can be repaired by at most one possible one-bit change: choose any failed literal, and the only eligible change must flip its bit. The probability of that change is at most $1/2$. The restored match, hence ownership, has conditional probability $p_j\le 2^{-f_j}$ by block independence. The expected $f_j$ of the restored owner is at most $\sum_j p_j(-\log_2 p_j)\le\log_2(J+1)$. Choose a uniform block and condition its latent mode to be $y$, at a factor of two. This is exactly the one-mode marginal of the contextual restoration, with that block as the served index. The probability the restored owner fails when it is made active again is bounded as claimed.

Consider an event with different full owners on the two active rows, and suppose the earlier owner is uncharged. That rule matches its own active row and survives inactivation of its served pair with probability at least $\alpha^2/(4t^2)$, conditional on the coupled active rows. At an endpoint where the address query permits several valid addresses, use a second matching balanced word. At least an $\alpha/t$ fraction of the original zero positions are free to be raised: half the differences of the two equally weighted words are zeros of the original word that differ from the second. A raise among them preserves the match. At an endpoint where the query permits only one valid address, being uncharged means the query does not test activation one there, so clearing activation preserves the match. The two endpoint changes are independent, giving the stated survival bound, also valid for a default. On a surviving restoration the corresponding restored owner is at most the earlier active owner, hence comes before the other active owner. The restored owners cannot then both agree and match their respective active rows: a common restored owner would violate first ownership on the other active row. By the two restoration estimates, the probability of different full active owners with earlier one uncharged is at most

$$
\frac{4t^2}{\alpha^2}\left(\frac{8\ln(J+1)}{m}+\frac{4\log_2(J+1)}{m}\right).
$$

If the earlier of two different full active owners is charged, the charged owners must disagree. Indeed the earlier full owner is itself the charged owner of its row, and cannot match the row with the later full owner. The bound from Section 4.3 therefore applies to this second case. Both rows being classified correctly implies different full owners, since their labels differ. By the union bound this joint correctness event has probability at least $1-2\operatorname{err}(L)$ under the ideal law. Hence

$$
\operatorname{err}(L)\ge\tfrac12-5\zeta-o(1)-O_\alpha\!\left(t^2\log(J+1)/m\right).
$$

Here $\log(J+1)=O(\log z)$ uniformly in $J\le k2^t$, since $\log z=\Theta(t^2)$, $H$ is fixed and $\log m=O(\log t)$. Increase the fixed $C_m$ so the last term is at most $\zeta$ for all sufficiently large $v$. This yields error at least $1/2-6\zeta-o(1)$ against **all** lists within the length cap, not only lists resembling the completeness witness.

## 5. Deterministic enumeration and the two complexity conclusions

The ideal distribution has finite support. To enumerate it with a common denominator, only degree-dependent copy choices require an approximation. With group occurrence count $M\ge v$, generate each degree-copy index by reducing a uniform integer in $[M^3]$ modulo its requested degree. Every copy still appears; its distribution changes by $O(M^{-2})$ in total variation since the degree is at most $M$. The combined variation is $O(mt/M^2)=o(1)$ by coupling the individual draws, including those in the inactive blocks. Thus the consistency and YES witness hold exactly, and the NO gap loses only $o(1)$. All other choices are uniform on explicitly enumerable sets or given by fixed rational weights (the jitter atom weights can be scaled to fixed integers). Use a common multiple of the two baseline slice sizes, their product sufficing, with $O(t)$ bits. The star choices use the same denominator at every endpoint because the balanced words have exactly $tn$ zeros. Enumerate every index of the resulting uniform product, allowing repetitions of the Boolean rows. No sampling is performed by the reduction.

Each pair needs $O_\varepsilon(t(n+\log(v+2)))=O_\varepsilon(t^2)$ bits of enumeration index: each group draws from a polynomial-size game and its addresses, and all field alphabets and jitter denominators are fixed. Baseline and star draws fit in this bound. Since $m=O_\varepsilon(t^4)$, the full sample has $\ln(N+2)=O_\varepsilon(t^6)$. The Boolean dimension is $O_\varepsilon(mt(n+1))=O_\varepsilon(t^6)$. If needed, replicate every index the same number of times until $N\ge2^d$; this preserves the empirical law and the size bound. The code search, the enumeration and all verifications are deterministic quasipolynomial-time operations in $v$. Repetitions are genuinely part of the explicit sample size. No promise of uniform full-cube support is made.

Take $\zeta<(1/2-\varepsilon)/20$ and fix a rational threshold $\tau$ strictly between $\varepsilon$ and $1/2-6\zeta$. For all sufficiently large $v$ the constructed instance has the following gap:

- **YES:** $\operatorname{OPT}_k(S)=0$.
- **NO:** every list with at most $k2^t$ paid rules and one default has empirical error greater than $\tau$. In particular $\operatorname{OPT}_k(S)>\tau$ in NO, so NO inputs need not satisfy the learner's promise.

Choose $c_\varepsilon>0$ small enough that $c_\varepsilon(\ln(N+2))^{1/6}\le (\ln 2)t/2$ on the reduction for large $v$. Consequently the stretched-exponential allowance in the theorem fits the tested cap $k2^t$ (even with any additional fixed prefactor for sufficiently large $v$). So does every fixed $A k(\log_2(N+2))^C$.

Run the supposed algorithm for its promised quasipolynomial time bound, enforced by a clock also outside the promise. Accept SAT exactly when its output has valid native syntax, at most $k2^t$ paid rules, one default and empirical error at most $\tau$, checked exactly by first-match evaluation on the indexed sample. The finitely many smaller SAT input lengths can be handled by exhaustive search. The total time remains quasipolynomial in $v$.

- **Deterministic:** On YES the algorithm must produce a passing list; on NO no passing list exists. This puts SAT, and therefore NP, in $\mathrm{QP}$.
- **Randomized:** On YES the joint accuracy and length guarantee gives a passing list with probability at least $2/3$. On NO **every coin outcome** is rejected, even if the learner makes no guarantee outside the promise. This puts SAT, and therefore NP, in one-sided $\mathrm{RQP}$. The reduction itself is deterministic; the learner is the only source of coins.

This proves both claims. The deterministic hypothesis alone does not give the unrestricted randomized conclusion by this argument. It does rule out randomized learners using at most $(\log(N+2))^{O(1)}$ worst-case coin bits, because all their coin tapes can then be enumerated in deterministic quasipolynomial time and the same verifier used. Conversely, neither lower bound establishes hardness for the separate case of a uniform, fully labeled Boolean cube. The statements concern arbitrary consistent indexed samples, and do not invoke ETH or rETH.
